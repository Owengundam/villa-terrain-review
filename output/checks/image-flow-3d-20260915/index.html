Calculation and assumptions

Viewpoint: centre of the front façade, at pad level + eye height. Villas are solid vertical extrusions of their actual footprints. A front building whose roof is strictly below the viewing villa’s floor plate (its adjusted pad level) is excluded from both overall and central obstruction. Roofs at or above the floor plate are evaluated normally. No fixed pad-drop threshold is used.

The horizontal window is ±15°. The central window is ±5° horizontally, from −2.5° to +2.5° vertically, centred on the 0° horizon. If the overall vertical window is narrower on either side, the central box shrinks equally on both sides to remain symmetric. The central requirement measures clear area, not a continuous opening. Default central blockage must be below 25%; exactly 25% fails.

Obstruction is measured as a fraction of horizontal-angle × vertical-angle area, with overlapping silhouettes counted once. Conservative 0.25° horizontal columns enclose each silhouette; narrow obstructions may be slightly overcounted. Pad fitting and removal use local search, not a maximum-count proof.

Defaults are study assumptions, not surveyed floor or roof heights. Eye height is measured from the pad; include any upper-floor offset here. Terrain, trees, windows and roof shape are not occluders in this building-only model. Terrain still supplies pad references and downhill orientation. Strict side clearance and existing geometry remain unchanged.

Terrain editing: each contour line is simplified to at most 10 control points, which you can drag in the terrain panel. OK saves the edited lines and reports the landmass cut/fill against the shipped terrain — nothing else. Planar fitting then faces every uphill arrow downhill, rotates every long axis further than the perpendicular tolerance off the local contour normal onto it, and slides villas to repair the clearance and boundary conflicts that re-aiming disturbs; the downhill sense comes from the contour levels (an arrow may not oppose the direction of the nearest lower contour, which is what the elevation labels show), not from the interpolated field between lines. 3D view fitting then adapts pads and activation for views. Elevation labels stay fixed; dragging a line never changes its labelled height.
